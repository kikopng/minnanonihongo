DRAFT: Minna no Nihongo Lesson 18 Study Notes

1. Overview & Core Objectives

Lesson 18 introduces fundamental structures for expressing personal abilities,
possibilities, personal interests/hobbies, and temporal sequencing using the
plain non-past form (commonly called the Dictionary Form or 辞書形 - jishokei).

By working through these notes, you will master:

  - Converting Group 1, Group 2, and Group 3 verbs into their Dictionary Form.
  - Nominalizing action verbs using the formal noun こと (koto) to allow verbs to
    occupy noun slots.
  - Expressing inherent abilities and circumstantial possibilities using 〜ができます
    (*~ ga dekimasu*) and 〜ことができます (*~ koto ga dekimasu*).
  - Describing hobbies and professions using nominalized verb clauses.
  - Sequencing actions and identifying elapsed time using 〜前に (*~ mae ni*).
  - Understanding key pragmatic differences between capability, asking
    permission, and making requests.

2. Core Vocabulary & Detailed Nuances

Verbs

  - できます [できる] (dekimasu / dekiru) — Group 2
      - Meaning: can do; be able to do; be ready.
      - Grammar Note: Although it ends in an [-i] sound preceding masu, this is
        an irregular/Group 2 verb. Its dictionary form is できる (dekiru).
      - Particle Usage: Takes the particle が (ga) for the skill or object of
        ability (e.g., 日本語ができます).
  - 洗います [洗う] (araimasu / arau) — Group 1
      - Meaning: to wash.
      - Example: 食事の前に、手を洗います (Shokuji no mae ni, te o araimasu - I wash my
        hands before meals).
  - 弾きます [弾く] (hikimasu / hiku) — Group 1
      - Meaning: to play (a stringed or keyboard instrument, e.g., piano,
        guitar, violin).
      - Instrument Note: Japanese uses different verbs for musical instruments:
          - 弾きます (hikimasu): string instruments & keyboards (piano, guitar).
          - 吹きます (fukimasu): wind/brass instruments (flute, trumpet).
          - 叩きます (tatakimasu): percussion instruments (drums).
  - 歌います [歌う] (utaimasu / utau) — Group 1
      - Meaning: to sing.
      - Example: 日本の歌を歌います (Nihon no uta o utaimasu - I sing Japanese songs).
  - 集めます [集める] (atsumemasu / atsumeru) — Group 2
      - Meaning: to collect; to gather together.
      - Example: ポケモンカードを集めています (Pokemon kādo o atsumete imasu - I collect /
        have been collecting Pokémon cards).
  - 捨てます [捨てる] (sutemasu / suteru) — Group 2
      - Meaning: to throw away; to discard.
      - Example: ごみを捨てます (Gomi o sutemasu - I throw away the trash).
  - 換えます [換える] (kaemasu / kaeru) — Group 2
      - Meaning: to exchange; to convert; to swap.
      - Kanji Nuance: 換える refers specifically to bartering, exchanging currency,
        or swapping one item for another of equal value (e.g., currency
        exchange: 円をドルに換える). Do not confuse with 変える (kaeru - to alter/change
        status) or 替える (kaeru - to replace a worn-out component).
  - 運転します [運転する] (untenshimasu / unten suru) — Group 3
      - Meaning: to drive (a vehicle/machinery).
      - Noun form: 運転 (unten - driving).
  - 予約します [予約する] (yoyakushimasu / yoyaku suru) — Group 3
      - Meaning: to reserve; to book.
      - Noun form: 予約 (yoyaku - reservation/booking).
      - Example: たかしで予約があります (Takashi de yoyaku ga arimasu - I have a
        reservation under the name Takashi).
  - お祈りします [お祈りする] (oinorishimasu / oinori suru) — Group 3
      - Meaning: to pray.
      - Noun form: お祈り (oinori - prayer).
      - Example: 教会でお祈りします (Kyōkai de oinorishimasu - I pray at church).

Nouns

  - ピアノ (piano) — piano.
  - 〜メートル (*~ mētoru*) — ~ meter(s) (counter/unit).
  - 現金 (genkin) — cash.
      - Collocation: 現金のみ (genkin nomi - cash only).
  - 趣味 (shumi) — hobby; personal pastime.
  - 日記 (nikki) — diary; journal.
      - Collocation: 日記を書きます (nikki o kakimasu - to write in a diary).
  - 社長 (shachō) — company president; CEO.
  - 動物 (dōbutsu) — animal (literally "moving thing").
  - 馬 (uma) — horse.
  - インターネット (intānetto) — the Internet.
      - Common abbreviation: ネット (netto).

Adverbs, Conjunctions & Set Phrases

  - 特に (tokuni) — especially; particularly.
      - Positive usage: 日本料理の中で特にそばが好きです (Nihon ryōri no naka de tokuni soba ga
        suki desu - Among Japanese foods, I especially like soba).
      - Negative usage: 今日は特に何もしませんでした (Kyō wa tokuni nanimo shimasen deshita -
        Today I didn't do anything in particular).
  - 是非 [ぜひ] (zehi) — by all means; definitely; certainly.
      - Used to express strong desire, enthusiasm, or to reinforce a polite
        invitation or request.
      - Example: また遊びましょう。→ はい、ぜひ！ (Mata asobimashō. -> Hai, zehi! - Let's hang
        out again. -> Yes, absolutely!).
  - なかなか (nakanaka) — quite / not easily.
      - With Affirmative: means "more than expected" or "quite good/tasty".
          - Cultural Caution: Saying これはなかなかおいしいです (Kore wa nakanaka oishii
            desu) to someone who cooked for you can sound unintentionally rude
            or patronizing, as it implies: "This is surprisingly good (even
            though I didn't expect much)."
      - With Negative: means "not easily," "hard to accomplish," or "by no
        means."
          - Example: 日本ではなかなかメキシコ料理を食べることができません (Nihon de wa nakanaka Mekishiko
            ryōri o taberu koto ga dekimasen - In Japan, you cannot easily
            find/eat Mexican food).
  - 本当ですか (hontō desu ka) — Really? / Is that true?

3. Verb Morphology: The Dictionary Form (辞書形)

What is the Dictionary Form?

The Dictionary Form (辞書形 - jishokei) is the citation form of a verb used in
dictionaries. Linguistically, it represents the plain (informal) non-past
affirmative form:

  - Polite style (丁寧語 - teineigo): 行きます (ikimasu)
  - Plain style (普通形 - futsūkei): 行く (iku)

Beyond casual speech with close friends, the dictionary form serves as an
essential building block inside complex clauses (such as nominalization,
expressing capability, expressing intentions, and setting chronological order).

Verb Categorization & Conversion Rules

  - Group 3: Irregular Verbs (不規則動詞)

      - します (shimasu - to do) → する (suru)
          - Applies to all noun compound verbs: 運転します → 運転する, 勉強します → 勉強する.
      - 来ます (kimasu - to come) → 来る (kuru)
          - Pronunciation Change Warning: Note that the kanji reading changes
            from ki- to ku-.

  - Group 2: Ichidan Verbs (一段動詞)

      - Verbs ending in the [-e] vowel sound before masu, plus a small set of
        single-mora or exception verbs ending in [-i] sound before masu.
      - Conversion Rule: Drop 〜ます (~masu) and attach 〜る (~ru).
          - 食べます (tabemasu) → 食べる (taberu)
          - 教えます (oshiemasu) → 教える (oshieru)
          - あげます (agemasu) → あげる (ageru)
          - 見ます (mimasu) → 見る (miru)
          - できます (dekimasu) → できる (dekiru)

  - Group 1: Godan Verbs (五段動詞)

      - Verbs ending in the [-i] vowel sound immediately preceding masu.
      - Conversion Rule: Shift the final syllable from the い-line (-i) to the
        corresponding う-line (-u) on the kana chart, and drop masu.
          - [-ki] → [-ku]: 行きます (ikimasu) → 行く (iku)
          - [-ki] → [-ku]: 弾きます (hikimasu) → 弾く (hiku)
          - [-gi] → [-gu]: 泳ぎます (oyogimasu) → 泳ぐ (oyogu)
          - [-shi] → [-su]: 話します (hanashimasu) → 話す (hanasu)
          - [-chi] → [-tsu]: 立ちます (tachimasu) → 立つ (tatsu)
          - [-ni] → [-nu]: 死にます (shinimasu) → 死ぬ (shinu)
          - [-bi] → [-bu]: 遊びます (asobimasu) → 遊ぶ (asobu)
          - [-mi] → [-mu]: 飲みます (nomimasu) → 飲む (nomu)
          - [-ri] → [-ru]: 取ります (torimasu) → 取る (toru)
          - [-i] → [-u]: 買います (kaimasu) → 買う (kau)
          - [-i] → [-u]: 歌います (utaimasu) → 歌う (utau)

4. Nominalization with こと (Verb [Dict] + こと)

The Role of こと (koto)

In Japanese, verbs cannot be directly attached to case particles like が (ga), を
(o), or standard copular structures like 〜は [X] です (*~ wa [X] desu*) if [X]
requires a noun.

To convert an entire action phrase into an abstract noun clause ("the act of
doing..."), attach the formal noun こと (koto) directly to the Dictionary Form of
the verb:

  - Verb (Dictionary form) + こと = Noun Phrase
  - 食べます (tabemasu / to eat) → 食べる (taberu) → 食べること (taberu koto / eating; the
    act of eating).

Common Sentence Patterns Using Nominalization

1. Expressing Likes and Dislikes

  - Structure: [Action clause: Verb (Dict) + こと] が 好きです / 嫌いです
  - Plain noun: お寿司が好きです (Sushi ga suki desu - I like sushi).
  - Nominalized verb: お寿司を食べることが、好きです (Sushi o taberu koto ga, suki desu - I
    like eating sushi).
  - Dislike example: コーヒーを飲むことが、嫌いです (Kōhī o nomu koto ga, kirai desu - I
    dislike drinking coffee).

2. Defining One's Occupation / Duty

  - Structure: 私の仕事は [Verb (Dict) + こと] です
  - Example A: 私の仕事は、英語を教えることです (Watashi no shigoto wa, Eigo o oshieru koto desu
    - My job is teaching English).
  - Example B: 私の仕事は、家を売ることです (Watashi no shigoto wa, ie o uru koto desu - My
    job is selling houses).

3. Describing One's Hobby

  - Structure: 私の趣味は [Verb (Dict) + こと] です
  - Example A: 私の趣味は、アニメを見ることです (Watashi no shumi wa, anime o miru koto desu -
    My hobby is watching anime).
  - Example B: 私の趣味は、写真を撮ることです (Watashi no shumi wa, shashin o toru koto desu -
    My hobby is taking photographs).
  - Example C: 母の趣味は、ピアノを弾くことです (Haha no shumi wa, piano o hiku koto desu - My
    mother's hobby is playing the piano).

5. Expressing Ability and Possibility: 〜ができます

A. Pattern 1: Noun + が できます

Used when a noun inherently contains an activity, skill, language, or sport.

  - Structure: [Noun] が できます (negative: が できません)
  - Particle Rule: The object of ability marks with が, not を.
  - Examples:
      - 私は日本語ができます (Watashi wa Nihongo ga dekimasu - I can do/speak Japanese).
      - 料理ができます (Ryōri ga dekimasu - I can cook).
      - ここでサッカーができません (Koko de sakkā ga dekimasen - You cannot play soccer
        here).

B. Pattern 2: Verb (Dict) + こと + が できます

Used when expressing specific actions or longer verb phrases.

  - Structure: [Verb Phrase (Dictionary form)] + ことが できます
  - Inherent Ability Examples:
      - 私は泳ぐことができます (Watashi wa oyogu koto ga dekimasu - I can swim).
      - 車を運転することができます (Kuruma o unten suru koto ga dekimasu - I can drive a
        car).
      - 色々な日本料理を作ることができます (Iroirona Nihon ryōri o tsukuru koto ga dekimasu - I
        can make various kinds of Japanese dishes).
      - お酒を飲むことができません (Osake o nomu koto ga dekimasen - I cannot drink alcohol).
  - Circumstantial Possibility Examples (actions possible under certain external
    conditions/locations):
      - このホテルでインターネットを使うことができます (Kono hoteru de intānetto o tsukau koto ga
        dekimasu - You can use the internet at this hotel).
      - カードで払うことができます (Kādo de harau koto ga dekimasu - You can pay by card).

C. Asking Questions & Replying

  - Question: 英語を話すことができますか (Eigo o hanasu koto ga dekimasu ka - Can you speak
    English?)
      - Affirmative Reply:
          - はい、話すことができます (Hai, hanasu koto ga dekimasu - Yes, I can speak it).
          - はい、できます (Hai, dekimasu - Yes, I can).
      - Negative Reply:
          - いいえ、話すことができません (Iie, hanasu koto ga dekimasen - No, I cannot speak
            it).
          - いいえ、できません (Iie, dekimasen - No, I cannot).
  - Wh- Questions:
      - 何語を話すことができますか (Nanigo o hanasu koto ga dekimasu ka - What languages can
        you speak?)
      - どこで着物を買うことができますか (Doko de kimono o kau koto ga dekimasu ka - Where can I
        buy a kimono?)

D. Critical Pragmatic Nuances & Common Errors

  - Grammar Distinction (Preview of Lesson 27):

      - Japanese has two primary ways to express "can":
        1.  Verb (Dict) + ことができます (Introduced here in Lesson 18).
        2.  Potential Form verbs (e.g., 食べられる, 話せる — Introduced in Lesson 27).
      - Note: The verb できます itself is historically the potential form of the
        verb します.

  - WARNING: Do NOT use 〜ことができますか to ask for permission!

      - Incorrect: 質問することができますか？ (Shitsumon suru koto ga dekimasu ka?)
          - Why it's wrong: This asks about intellectual or physical capability
            ("Do I possess the capacity to formulate and utter a question?").
      - Correct: 質問してもいいですか？ (Shitsumon shite mo ii desu ka? - May I ask a
        question? / Using the 〜てもいいですか permission pattern from Lesson 15).

  - WARNING: Do NOT use 〜ことができますか to ask someone to do something!

      - Incorrect: お塩を取ることができますか？ (O-shio o toru koto ga dekimasu ka?)
          - Why it's wrong: This literally asks if the person is physically
            capable of reaching the salt, not whether they are willing to pass
            it.
      - Correct:
          - お塩を取ってください (O-shio o totte kudasai - Please pass the salt
            [Lesson 14]).
          - お塩を取っていただけませんか (O-shio o totte itadakemasen ka - Could you please
            pass the salt? [Polite request, Lesson 26]).

6. Temporal Sequence and Elapsed Time: 〜前に (まえに)

The phrase 前に (mae ni) expresses that an action or event occurs prior to another
point in time, or that a specific amount of time has elapsed since an event.

Pattern 1: Action Noun + の 前に

  - Structure: [Noun] + の前に、[Main Clause]
  - Requirement: The noun must represent an activity or event that inherently
    has a duration (e.g., 食事, 会議, 旅行, 仕事).
  - Examples:
      - 食事の前に、手を洗います (Shokuji no mae ni, te o araimasu - I wash my hands before
        meals).
      - 毎朝、仕事の前に、運動します (Maiasa, shigoto no mae ni, undō shimasu - Every morning,
        I exercise before work).
      - 日本旅行の前に、新しいかばんを買いたいです (Nihon ryokō no mae ni, atarashii kaban o kaitai
        desu - Before the trip to Japan, I want to buy a new bag).

Pattern 2: Verb (Dictionary Form) + 前に

  - Structure: [Verb 1 (Dictionary Form)] + 前に、[Verb 2 (Main Clause)]
  - Meaning: Before doing Verb 1, do Verb 2.
  - Examples:
      - ご飯を食べる前に、手を洗います (Gohan o taberu mae ni, te o araimasu - Before eating a
        meal, I wash my hands).
      - 寝る前に、この薬を飲んでください (Neru mae ni, kono kusuri o nonde kudasai - Please take
        this medicine before going to bed).
      - 私にキスする前に、歯を磨いてください (Watashi ni kisu suru mae ni, ha o migaite kudasai -
        Please brush your teeth before kissing me).

The Tense Consistency Rule (CRUCIAL)

  - In the structure [Verb 1] 前に、[Verb 2], Verb 1 ALWAYS remains in the non-past
    Dictionary Form, regardless of whether the entire sentence happened in the
    past or will happen in the future.
  - The tense of the sentence is determined only by the final predicate verb
    (Verb 2).
      - Present/Future: ご飯を食べる前に、手を洗います (Taberu mae ni, te o araimasu - Before I
        eat, I will wash my hands).
      - Past: ご飯を食べる前に、手を洗いました (Taberu mae ni, te o araimashita - Before I ate,
        I washed my hands).
      - Error to Avoid: Never say 食べた前に (tabeta mae ni). This is ungrammatical
        in standard Japanese.

Pattern 3: Elapsed Time Period + 前に

  - Structure: [Time Period / Duration] + 前に、[Main Clause]
  - Meaning: [Time Period] ago.
  - Crucial Particle Distinction: Do NOT place the particle の between the time
    quantity and 前に.
  - Examples:
      - 15分前に、手を洗いました (Jūgo-fun mae ni, te o araimashita - I washed my hands 15
        minutes ago).
      - 3年前に、この車を買いました (San-nen mae ni, kono kuruma o kaimashita - I bought this
        car 3 years ago).
      - 1ヶ月前に、初めて日本へ行きました (Ikkagetsu mae ni, hajimete Nihon e ikimashita - One
        month ago, I went to Japan for the first time).

7. Comparative Summary of Key Structures

Nominalizer Structures

  - Verb (Dict) + こと: Converts an action clause into an abstract noun phrase.
  - [Verb clause] ことが好きです: Identifies an action that one enjoys doing.
  - [Verb clause] ことが嫌いです: Identifies an action that one dislikes doing.
  - 私の趣味は [Verb clause] ことです: Defines one's hobby as an active pastime.
  - 私の仕事は [Verb clause] ことです: Defines one's occupation by the core duty
    performed.

Ability / Possibility Structures

  - [Noun] ができます: Expresses ability in a broad subject, language, or sport.
  - [Verb (Dict)] ことができます: Expresses ability or situational feasibility to
    perform a specific action.
  - Particle Warning: The object of the potential structure takes が; inside the
    sub-clause, the verb maintains its normal case particle (e.g., 本を読むこと が
    できます).

Temporal Sequencing with 前に

  - Noun + の 前に: Used with action/event nouns (e.g., 会議の前に). Requires の.
  - Verb (Dict) + 前に: Used with action verbs. The verb is always Dictionary
    form, never past tense (e.g., 出かける前に).
  - Time Period + 前に: Indicates time elapsed (e.g., 2日前に). Does not take の.
